The story

Same stones.
New life.

A pagliarola is a traditional rural stone shelter, built to last and never meant to impress. We kept what made it special—the hand-laid walls, the arched doorway, the timber above—and added only what a stay needs: a kitchen, a loft bed, a quiet bathroom and warm light.

Around Santo Stefano Quisquina

Beyond the door.

Pagliaroom sits in a hill village in the Sicani mountains. Start in the square, then follow the woods up to the hermitage.

Scheda · Village centre

Fontana di Piazza Castello

The village’s meeting point: a tiered stone fountain in the square locals call “Lu Casteddu”, with water spilling from bowl to bowl into a wide basin.

Where
Piazza Castello, in the centre of Santo Stefano Quisquina
Date
18th century
Form
Four-lobed basin, then a column rising through three bowls to a small figure on top
Setting
The square is named after a castle that no longer exists. The former baronial palace of the Ventimiglia family stands beside it, now a private house.
Tip
Hikers’ notes mention drinking water here in summer; check locally before you refill.

Good to know.

What is a pagliarola?

A traditional stone outbuilding of rural Sicily, historically used to store hay, tools and produce. Ours has been carefully restored as a stay.

How many people can stay?

The loft bed and the sofa nook suit couples and small groups. Tell us your group size in the form and we’ll confirm the best setup.

Is there a kitchen?

Yes—a full open kitchen with an island, induction hob, oven and sink, set against the stone wall.

How do I book?

Send the form above. It opens an email to us with your dates and details, and we’ll answer with availability and rates.
